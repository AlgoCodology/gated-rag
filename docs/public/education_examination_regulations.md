# Education and Examination Regulations (OER) 2025-2026

These regulations apply to all bachelor's and master's programmes of Home University. Faculty-specific supplements may add rules but cannot weaken the rules below.

## Chapter 1. General provisions

### Article 1.1 Definitions

- **ECTS:** European Credit Transfer System. One ECTS equals 28 hours of study. A full academic year is 60 ECTS.
- **Board of Examiners:** the independent body that safeguards the quality of examinations and decides on exemptions and fraud.
- **Resit:** a second opportunity to take an exam of a course for which the student has not obtained a pass grade.

### Article 1.2 Programme duration

A bachelor's programme comprises 180 ECTS (three years). A master's programme comprises 60 ECTS (one year) or 120 ECTS (two years), as stated in the programme supplement.

## Chapter 2. Admission

### Article 2.1 Bachelor's programmes

Admission requires a Dutch VWO diploma or an equivalent qualification. Students with a HBO propaedeutic diploma are admitted to the second year of related programmes. Applicants who are 21 or older without a diploma can take the entrance assessment ("21+ toets") in March.

### Article 2.2 Language requirements

For English-taught programmes, applicants must show proficiency with one of: IELTS academic overall 6.5 (no sub-score below 6.0), TOEFL iBT 90 (no sub-score below 20), or Cambridge C1 Advanced grade C or higher. For Dutch-taught programmes, a Dutch NT2 Programme II certificate is required.

## Chapter 3. Examinations

### Article 3.1 Grading

Grades are given on a scale from 1 to 10, rounded to one decimal. The pass mark is **5.5**. The final grade for a course is only rounded to a whole number, or to a half, in the cases defined by the programme supplement. Grades below 5.5 can be compensated only if the programme supplement allows compensation.

### Article 3.2 Number of attempts

Each student has two attempts per academic year for every course: the regular exam and one resit. A student who has passed a course may not retake it to improve the grade, except in the *honours resit* described in Article 3.3.

### Article 3.3 Honours resit

A student who passed a course with a grade between 6.0 and 7.5 may use the honours resit once during the whole bachelor's programme, if the course coordinator agrees. The higher grade counts.

### Article 3.4 Registration and absence

Students must register for an exam at least 15 calendar days before the exam date. A student who is absent from a registered exam without a valid reason loses the attempt. Valid reasons are illness, the death of a close relative, or official top-athlete obligations. They must be reported to the study adviser within 5 working days.

### Article 3.5 Validity period

Exam results remain valid for **seven years**. After that the Board of Examiners decides whether a student needs to retake the course.

### Article 3.6 Extra time and facilities

Students with a documented disability, such as dyslexia, ADHD or a visual impairment, are entitled to an extension of 25 percent of the exam time, or a different form of facility if recommended by the student counsellor. Requests must be submitted at least 4 weeks before the exam.

## Chapter 4. Fraud and plagiarism

### Article 4.1 Definition

Fraud is any action by which a student makes it impossible, fully or partly, to correctly assess their knowledge, insight or skills. This includes cheating during an exam, using unauthorised aids, plagiarism, collusion, and the submission of work produced by generative AI without permission from the course coordinator.

### Article 4.2 Sanctions

The Board of Examiners decides on sanctions. Possible sanctions:

1. A reprimand;
2. A zero grade for the exam or assignment concerned;
3. Exclusion from the next exam or resit of the course, for at most one year;
4. In serious cases and after advice from the Executive Board, permanent termination of enrolment.

### Article 4.3 Procedure

The student is always given the opportunity to be heard before a sanction is imposed. The Board of Examiners decides within **10 working days** after the hearing.

## Chapter 5. Binding Study Advice (BSA)

### Article 5.1 Standard

Bachelor's students must obtain at least **45 of the 60 ECTS** of the first year (propaedeutic phase). A student who does not reach this threshold receives a negative binding study advice and must stop the programme. The student cannot re-enrol in the same programme for the next three academic years.

### Article 5.2 Personal circumstances

If a student could not meet the standard due to personal circumstances (illness, family circumstances, pregnancy, functional impairment, or membership of a governing body), the BSA can be postponed or waived. These circumstances must be reported to the student counsellor as soon as they arise, and in any case before 1 March.

## Chapter 6. Appeals and complaints

### Article 6.1 Appeal against exam decisions

A student can appeal against a decision of an examiner or the Board of Examiners to the Examination Appeals Board (*College van Beroep voor de Examens*) within **six weeks** after the decision was communicated. The appeal is free of charge.

### Article 6.2 Inspection of exams

Students have the right to inspect their graded exam within 30 days after the results are published. The inspection session is organised by the examiner.

## Chapter 7. Graduation

### Article 7.1 Degree and honours

A student graduates when all components of the programme have been completed and the Board of Examiners has declared this. The predicate *cum laude* is awarded if the weighted average is at least **8.0**, no grade below 7.0 was obtained, and the programme was completed within the nominal duration plus one year. The predicate *summa cum laude* requires a weighted average of at least **9.0**.
